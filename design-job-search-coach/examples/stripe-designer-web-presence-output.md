## Fit read

The posting describes a marketing/web-storytelling designer who also thinks in systems — closer to product-marketing design than pure brand or pure product. Your shape is inverted: five years of B2B SaaS *product* design with a component-library systems background, plus two shipped marketing sites on the side. The systems fluency and Figma component-library experience map directly onto "partner with other designers to maintain and evolve systems." The storytelling/conversion half — landing pages that turn interest into action — is the side project, not the day job. That's a real gap, not a fatal one: broadcast the systems-and-craft signal loudly, and let the two marketing sites carry the storytelling case (Kyle Zantos: candidates should broadcast their shape, not blur it).

## Evidence checklist

- **Five-second surface pass** — prepare 2–3 web/landing-page screens (ideally the marketing sites) that read clean at a glance: layout, type, color, an opinion. Prototype over deck (Jared Tredly).
- **Visual craft as differentiator** — since your daily work is B2B SaaS UI, actively curate for taste that isn't visible in that work — the marketing sites are your evidence here (Chris Andrews).
- **Conviction / attributable decisions** — be ready to say which layout, copy, or system calls on the marketing sites were yours vs. a client's/collaborator's (Lauren LoPrete; Oscar Lozano).
- **Judgment and leverage** — have a story about a component-library decision you pushed back on or rejected, and what it unlocked for other designers (Genny Dee).
- **Code fluency** — posting lists this as *preferred*, not required, and only asks you to "speak fluently with engineers," not write code — your read-not-write front-end comfort matches this exactly (Conor Muirhead: code isn't a bar everywhere; this posting confirms it's optional here).
- **Open the actual Figma** — be ready to walk your component library and one marketing site end to end, live (Dylan Widick).
- Take-home/paid task: not mentioned in this posting — not asked for here.

(Kevin Twohy's framing, adapted since this isn't a founding role but has a similarly wide band)
2. Pay is listed as base salary only — what does total comp (equity, bonus) typically look like at the level you're hiring for?
3. "Apply for roles matching where you live" plus the 35-mile rule — how does that get verified, and does it affect which level or team I'd be considered for if I'm fully remote?

## Interview prep

- Expect a portfolio walkthrough where they ask you to open the real Figma file, not just present slides (Dylan).
- Some processes now include a live build or prototyping round instead of a static deck — prepare to work in the room (Ved).
- Even for a role like this, a paid or time-boxed take-home is possible — don't assume none will appear just because this posting doesn't mention one (Dan Billson).
- Ask your three questions early in the process, not just at the end (Anjali).
- If you know anyone at Stripe or in the Presence/marketing design world, a warm path in beats a cold application (Ethan).
