## Fit read (150 words max)

They're describing a solo generalist: 80% product IC across three surfaces, 20% brand/identity, plus building the design function from scratch. Kevin Twohy's founding-role test cuts against the title here — reporting to the CEO and shaping how design *works* leans capital-F founding, but "who this isn't for" explicitly excludes people who want to manage on day one, and the JD never mentions equity level, decision authority, or a path to building a team. That reads more like a well-branded first-designer role. Your shape — 8 years, 2 leading a small team, own front-end prototyping, B2B SaaS — mismatches on two explicit asks: they want *consumer* mobile-first social/payments experience and *visual identity/brand* craft, neither of which B2B SaaS portfolios typically demonstrate. Your leadership experience is a plus they don't need yet, per the "not for" line.

## Evidence checklist

- **Five-second surface pass** — this posting explicitly leans on visual/brand craft ("strong graphic and visual craft," "evolve visual identity," "read as one family"). Prepare a portfolio opener that shows taste and a point of view fast, not process walls (Jared Tredly).
- **Personal project / unconstrained taste** — since your work is B2B SaaS, a self-directed consumer or social/payments concept piece would directly address the biggest gap between your history and their brief (Chris Andrews).
- **Conviction on what's yours** — with no PM/researcher here, be ready to say which product and brand calls were yours end-to-end (Lauren LoPrete).
- **Judgment/leverage** — what you rejected and what system you built for a small team; your 2 years leading translates well here even though the role itself is solo (Genny Dee).
- **Code as differentiator, not bar** — posting lists HTML/CSS as "nice to have," not required; your front-end prototyping is a bonus signal, not a screening requirement (Conor Muirhead).
- **Paid take-home norms** — not stated here; posting asks only for a portfolio + written note, no task described (Oscar Lozano) — flag as "not asked for here" rather than assume one is coming.
- **Walk the actual file** — not stated whether interviews include a live Figma walkthrough; prepare one regardless given the sole-designer, high-ownership framing (Dylan Widick).

## Interview prep

- Expect to open your actual portfolio/Figma and walk it end to end, not just present slides (Dylan Widick — one account, not a universal rule).
- A live, build-something-working round is increasingly common even where not advertised; have a prototype ready to drive live (Ved — one account).
- Ask your own questions early rather than saving them for the end, and have one real personal story ready (Anjali — one account).
- No take-home is stated in this posting, but some teams add one mid-process even without listing it (Dan Billson — one account).
- Showing up with a self-directed concept for their specific product gap (consumer/brand) may matter more than another cold application (Ethan — one account).
