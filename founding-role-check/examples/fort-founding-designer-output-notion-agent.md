## Which job this is

Points to capital F
- Very early company: “YC W26” and a “small founding team.”
- Broad founding mandate: “define how Fort looks, feels, and communicates across product, brand, and growth.”
- Organizational leverage: “resources to work with or hire others into the design team at Fort.”
- Meaningful cash and equity are stated. “You should be compensated for that in cash and equity very well.” (Kevin Twohy)

Points toward first designer with a founding title
- The practical center of gravity is still design production, “focusing on the app” plus marketing, social, email, packaging, and community.
- Most authority described is over creative output: “Set standards for typography, layout, hierarchy, color, imagery, motion, and art direction.”
- Business strategy, pricing, roadmap authority, and company-level decision rights are not stated.
- The founders are engineers, but who has been designing the product and brand is not stated. Team shape is a question, not a verdict.

This looks closer to a capital F role because of the company stage, breadth, hiring potential, and compensation. The answer changes if “own” means producing every design surface while founders retain product, positioning, and business decisions. Kevin Twohy’s capital F version expects you “to figure out not just the product, not just the brand, but really be thinking about the business and helping steer it.”

## Ask the founder

- “In my first eight months, what decisions are gonna be put under my purview?” “You'll learn a lot from the answer to that.” (Kevin Twohy)
- Have you worked with designers before? “The best signal would be is if they've worked with other designers.” (Tom Johnson)
- Who has been doing product and brand design so far, and what will that person hand over? “They have to give you that purview, that ability to steer and direct the product.” (Tom Johnson)
- What is the riskiest part of Fort’s business? If founders “haven't thought through the potential failure states, that's a red flag for me.” (Ridd)
- What does excellent design look like to each founder? Trust is “alignment plus execution,” beginning with “understanding what the bar in your founder's mind actually is.” (Jared Erondu)
- Advice: Which experience requirement is accurate, 3+ or 5+, and is New York genuinely supported or an exceptional arrangement?

Across the answers, watch for how disagreement is handled. “People underestimate kindness in these collaborations.” (Tommy Geoco)

## Check yourself

Your background touches product UI, data-heavy B2B work, consumer onboarding, growth surfaces, and small brand refreshes. It does not answer these questions for you:

- Variety: “if avatars for social need to be made, you're gonna be making them. And it might not be on the day that you wanna make them.” (Kevin Twohy)
- Ambiguity: “In a lot of early stage roles, it's your job to come up with both the problem and the solution.” (Kevin Twohy)
- Isolation: “how long are you gonna be comfortable being the only person on the team in that design function?” (Kevin Twohy)
- Identity: “how much do you want your company and your work to be a part of your identity?” (Kevin Twohy)
- Longevity: The role “is only the same for, say, eighteen months to two years.” “Where do you wanna be in year three, and is this role in this company and this founder helping you aim there?” (Kevin Twohy)
- Discarded work: “If someone were to tell me that months of work needed to be thrown away tomorrow does that fill me with dread or do I accept that loss and roll up my sleeves and move on?” (Claire Taylor)

Three experience views:

- “In general, I don't think that being a founding designer is well suited to folks who are early in their career. There are exceptions, but in general, I think the support system that you need to be set up for success just isn't there.” (Josh Armantrout)
- It is “a role that I would not recommend to the vast majority of junior designers”; it suits “folks who are at the senior level or higher, like staff, or really scrappy people who are willing to suffer and chew glass.” (Flora Guo)
- Knowing the industry or problem deeply “makes you uniquely qualified to be a founding designer too,” even with “a couple years of experience.” (Ridd)

## What the job really includes

The posting openly includes product, brand, growth, social, email, packaging, art direction, founder content, and investor updates.

It leaves less explicit:

- Cofounder-like responsibility: “You will be treated more like a cofounder than an employee.” (Jacob)
- Unplanned design work where “there's no priority other than urgent.” (Jacob)
- Being the product’s public voice: “You've gotta do marketing. You've gotta do social media stuff. You've gotta be a voice of the product.” (Dan Petty)
- UX writing: “You're not gonna have a damn UX writer as a founding designer.” (Ridd)
- Prioritization under pressure: “Everything is your problem, but everything can't be your priority.” (Jared Erondu)

## What this skill cannot tell you

It cannot tell you whether to take the role, what the equity is worth, whether the founders will keep their word, what the days feel like, or anything marked “not stated.”

“I don't really think you can have a great founding designer without a great founding team.” (Andres Gonzalez) Only meeting the team answers that.
