## Ask the founder

1. "In my first eight months, what decisions are gonna be put under my purview?" "You'll learn a lot from the answer to that." (Kevin Twohy)
2. Have you worked with designers before? "the best signal would be is if they've worked with other designers." (Tom Johnson)
3. Who has been doing the design so far, and what will you hand over? "They have to give you that purview, that ability to steer and direct the product." (Tom Johnson)
4. What does good look like to you? Trust is "alignment plus execution," and alignment starts with "understanding what the bar in your founder's mind actually is." (Jared Erondu)
5. Gap: who do I report to, and who has the final say on brand and app decisions? Both are not stated.
6. Gap: is the bar 3+ or 5+ years, and how soon would you hire others into the design team?

Watch every answer for one thing: "People underestimate kindness in these collaborations." (Tommy Geoco)

## Check yourself

No score. Your note touches Variety, Ambiguity and Claire's playbook question. It also touches the experience views. I won't answer any of them for you.

**Kevin Twohy's five rubrics**
- Variety: "if avatars for social need to be made, you're gonna be making them. And it might not be on the day that you wanna make them."
- Ambiguity: "In a lot of early stage roles, it's your job to come up with both the problem and the solution."
- Isolation: "how long are you gonna be comfortable being the only person on the team in that design function?"
- Identity: "how much do you want your company and your work to be a part of your identity?"
- Longevity, which Kevin calls the most important: the role "is only the same for, say, eighteen months to two years." "Where do you wanna be in year three, and is this role in this company and this founder helping you aim there?"

**Claire Taylor**
- "If someone were to tell me that months of work needed to be thrown away tomorrow does that fill me with dread or do I accept that loss and roll up my sleeves and move on?"
- "Does the idea of writing the playbook rather than following one excite me?"
- "Does the idea of not having a design system scare you or excite you?"

**Dan Tase:** "You really need to love what you're building on a daily basis for this to be manageable."

**Experience: three views, no decision**
- Josh Armantrout: "In general, I don't think that being a founding designer is well suited to folks who are early in their career. There are exceptions, but in general, I think the support system that you need to be set up for success just isn't there."
- Flora Guo: "a role that I would not recommend to the vast majority of junior designers"; it suits "folks who are at the senior level or higher, like staff, or really scrappy people who are willing to suffer and chew glass."
- Ridd: if you know the industry or problem well, "that makes you uniquely qualified to be a founding designer too," even with "a couple years of experience."
